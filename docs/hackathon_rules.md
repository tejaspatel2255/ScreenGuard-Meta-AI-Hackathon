# Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon: rules and facts

Source: the participant guide and event page on Cerebral Valley, copied on Oct 27, 2026.
The Official Rules were not published when this was written. Check Cerebral Valley and Discord #announcements, then update this file. If this file and the Official Rules disagree, the Official Rules win.

## 1. Event basics

- Hosts: Meta and Cerebral Valley. Remote and worldwide.
- Kickoff: Mon Oct 26, 12:00 PM ET. Building period: Oct 27 to Nov 4, 2026.
- Submissions due: Wed Nov 4, 9:00 PM ET, through the Cerebral Valley platform. Late submissions are not considered.
- Tracks: Indie Developer (individual builders and students) and Startups. Every project enters one track. Our track: Indie Developer.
- Team size: maximum 4. Solo is allowed. Teams submit one project, and everyone who worked on it must be listed on the submission page.

## 2. Problem statements (the project must build toward one)

- Build for Your World: bring Meta's models into your domain. Solve a problem you know firsthand, free up time and mental space, and make something possible that was not before.
- Push the Frontier: use Meta's models to tackle challenges in software, robotics, manufacturing, energy, materials and beyond. Give a machine a new capability, accelerate a scientific experiment, or unlock a better way to understand and control a physical system.
- Our choice: Build for Your World.

## 3. Meta tools (the project must incorporate Meta's models)

- Through Meta Model API: Muse Spark series, Muse Wonder, Muse Image, Muse Voice Transcribe, SAM 3.1.
- Open-weights models: Muse Glimmer, Muse Spark Open.
- Coding agent: Muse Code (helps developers code faster).
- Our plan: Muse Spark (core), SAM 3.1 and Muse Voice Transcribe (supporting), optional Muse Spark Open via Baseten.

## 4. Rules

- Repositories must be public.
- New work only: what is presented must have been built during the event. Startups may build on existing products, but Indie projects should be entirely new.
- The demo must only highlight features, code and functionality built during the hackathon. Judges must be able to clearly identify what was created during the hackathon. Failing to do so means immediate disqualification at the judges' discretion.
- Banned projects: those that violate legal, ethical or platform policies, or use code, data or assets you do not have rights to.
- Duplicate applications, false identity information or spam are disqualified.

### Sample anti-projects (avoid)

- AI that gives medical advice (mental health advisor, skin blemish detector and similar)
- Basic RAG applications
- Simple image analyzers
- Simple "AI for Education" chatbots
- AI job application screener
- AI nutrition coach
- Personality analyzers
- Sports analyzers or coaches
- Anything political or involving war

## 5. Eligibility (event page)

- Open worldwide, except where local law prohibits participation or receiving an award.
- Not eligible: anyone targeted by US, EU, UN or UK trade sanctions or export controls, or organized or located in Belarus, China, Russia, Ukraine, Syria, or comprehensively sanctioned regions (Crimea, Donetsk, Luhansk, Cuba, North Korea, Iran).
- Not eligible: employees, contractors and interns of Meta and Cerebral Valley, government-affiliated individuals, their immediate families and people living in the same household.

## 8. Submission process

- Submit on the Cerebral Valley platform by Nov 4, 9:00 PM ET. One project per team.
- Required: a three-minute demo video showing the specific features, code and functionality built during the hackathon. The video is a primary artifact for judges, along with the code and the live product.
- Check before submitting: the repository is public, the video link is accessible, and everyone who worked on the project is listed.
- Each team appoints one Representative who submits.

## 9. Judging

- Stage 1 (pass or fail): the project reasonably fits a theme and reasonably used Meta Model API and/or Meta open-weights models.
- Stage 2: equally weighted rubric, each scored 0 to 10.
- Stage 3: the top 5 projects per track go to a final round where judges watch the video demos live and rank them with the same rubric.

| Category | What judges ask |
|---|---|
| Impact | Real-world potential. Who benefits and how much. Could people actually use it. Does it fit a problem statement. |
| Use of Meta AI Tooling | How creatively Meta's tooling is used. Beyond a basic integration. Surfacing capabilities that surprise the team at Meta. |
| Technical Depth and Execution | Sound, thoughtfully refined engineering with real craft. |
| Demo | Working, impressive demo that holds up live. A genuinely cool demo video. |
